web client · mockup · the reference for its look since 2026-11-21

One frontend, laid out for the width it has: a level at a time on a phone, side by side at a desk. Every frame on this page is the same code at a different width, and each one is live. Click around in them, and send a message in any thread to watch a scripted turn.

Nothing here talks to a gateway. What decides the look is one block at the top of this file: two wools, eight pigments, two faces and a stitch.

01At a desk

Agents and rooms, then everything one agent takes part in, then a thread. The lantern by an agent is lit while it works, in the list and on the tide above it.

Folded, the two lists are a strip of sigils, with each lantern and new mark still on it. The pointer brings the lists back over the thread until it leaves them.

02On a phone

The same three levels, one at a time, with no browser bar around them.

03The other time of day

The two wools trade places: the ground of one is the ink of the other. Here the agent's view is open, which puts the session as the agent sees it beside the conversation. On a phone the same button turns the thread over.

04When a room is busy

However many agents are at work in a thread, one of them is open and the others take a line each. However many wake-ups wait there, the dock shows the soonest and a count, and the rest open in place.

05An agent's home

The home tab is the agent's folder, to look through and not to change.

06When things are not fine

A view with no connection loses its color and says so. A thread you only read says why there is no input. A slash opens the commands, and /status answers above the input, for you alone.

07What it is made of

The studio's guardrails, applied to a surface that is read for hours: a warm dark that is never black, pigment that sits close to the ground, and pixels used as a quiet material.

Dusk navy heather

The ribbing of the sweater in the studio's refs: dark navy with warm flecks. Cream is the ink.

Day cream wool

The body of the same sweater. Navy is the ink, and the flecks turn to the blue of its clouds.

Pigments at dusk

Faded and matte, and each one means something. Most of a screen is ground and ink.

Pigments by day

The same pigments, deepened for paper. Each has a darker form for small text.

One stitch 2px

Every texture is drawn on one grid, the size of a stitch. Nothing glows and nothing blurs: where a value has to fade, it is dithered.

Marks

The terminal's marks, drawn on the stitch. A mark never stands without its word where the word matters: a failure says failed.

Sigils 12 × 12, one color

An agent's face is a bitmap small enough to keep as twelve lines of text. It holds at 12px beside a name, and as a relief on an empty screen.

Type the one choice still open

The file asks for the first pair. Your first look at this page was in the second, because the preview it opened in had no network to fetch a face. The switch at the top of the page flips every frame between them.

Atkinson Hyperlegible

Drawn for readers with low vision, so its letters are hard to mistake for each other. It is the same on every device.

The system's own

Whatever the device reads everything else in. It needs no network, and it is a different face on each kind of device.

Either way, the proportional face is for what a person or an agent wrote and the mono is for what a machine wrote: tool calls, paths, times, commands and the line of keys. No bitmap type: pixels are the material, and words stay crisp beside them.

Steps

Everything that moves, moves in steps. The tide is redrawn twice a second, the halo of a lantern has three frames, and the shrimp walks at the terminal's 180 ms. Reduced motion stops all of it.

At other sizes

The same marks in the terminal as it is today, on a small matte screen, and in a browser tab.

08What this decided, and what it leaves open

You took this page as the reference on 2026-11-21. These are the places where it chose something the plan hadn't said. They stand as drawn until you say otherwise.

Chosen here

  1. An agent's list is in two groups, your threads and elsewhere, from your note of 2026-11-17. The terminal's filter stays: everything, or yours.
  2. At a desk the agent's view sits beside the conversation. On a phone it turns the thread over, as Tab does in the terminal.
  3. The two lists fold into a strip of sigils, from a tablet's width up, on the button at the top of the rail or Ctrl+\, the key the terminal mockup folds on. The pointer, a tap or the focus brings them back over the thread, and they go when it leaves or a thread is picked. Each device remembers its own fold.
  4. Each agent has a pigment and a sigil. The terminal draws every agent in one color. Who draws a sigil, and whether it is a file in the agent's home, is open.
  5. The tide. One lantern for each agent, lit while it works, above the list of agents.
  6. An agent has three tabs: threads, triggers and home. Home is the agent's folder to look through, and the file that holds its token is not shown.
  7. A thread's dock keeps to a line for each kind of thing: who is working, and the soonest wake-up with a count of the rest. Where several agents work at once, one block of work is open and the others are a line each.
  8. A room you only read has a join button beside the command it stands for.
  9. A stale view loses its color and gets a dotted ground, under the sentence the terminal already says, with when it tries next.
  10. The line of keys stays at a desk, so every key is still findable. Three of the terminal's keys can't come along as they are: Tab moves focus in a browser, and Ctrl+O and Ctrl+T belong to the browser off the Mac. Here the agent's view is on Ctrl+. and the other two are buttons on the work.
